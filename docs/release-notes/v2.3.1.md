# MiWRT 2.3.1

A fix release for the Redmi AC2100: a router that moves to MiWRT from another OpenWrt with its settings keeps its Wi-Fi as it was, and what an update keeps no longer depends on `/etc/sysupgrade.conf`. The BE7000 image is the same as in 2.3.0 apart from the version number, so a BE7000 on 2.3.0 does not need this update.

## Fixed

- Redmi AC2100: **a router that comes from another OpenWrt with its settings keeps its Wi-Fi exactly as it was.** The first start used to set the country to GB on radios that had none, which changes the channels and transmit power allowed, and it turned on its own "MiWRT" networks when the old firmware had Wi-Fi switched off or open. Now a router with a root password, a Wi-Fi network with a password or one switched on counts as set up, and its Wi-Fi and its choice of software flow offloading are left alone. A router with none of these is treated as new, as before.
- Redmi AC2100: **what an update keeps no longer depends on `/etc/sysupgrade.conf`.** The two MiWRT lines there (which services are on, and the note that the first start is done) moved to `/lib/upgrade/keep.d/miwrt-ac2100`, which is part of the image. A router that came from another OpenWrt with its own edited `sysupgrade.conf` kept that file, and its next update then lost both.
- Both fixes were tried on a Redmi AC2100: from official OpenWrt 25.12.6 with the settings kept, and through the next update after that.

## Good to know

- BE7000, updating from 2.2.0: on a router where a package was ever added or removed, the update brings `ethtool-full`, `iperf3` and `tcpdump-mini` back from the feed. 2.3.0 took them out of the image, and the update cannot tell them from packages you added yourself. They take about 0.7 MB of the settings space; `apk del iperf3 tcpdump-mini` removes the last two if you do not want them. Leave `ethtool-full` installed: removing it would also hide the image's own `ethtool`.
- BE7000: the update from 2.2.0 to 2.3.0 was installed on a router with its own updater (`miwrt-update`, which the app and the update page also use). This was the first update on a real router that cleaned up the settings volume. Only an old copy of `/etc/banner` was moved aside. Every setting came through unchanged, and Wi-Fi, AdGuard Home, banIP, the app service and the VPN pages were all working after the boot.
